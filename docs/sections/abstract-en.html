Abstract

Ontology engineering is a complex, time-consuming, and error-prone process, and is particularly demanding in regulatory domains, where knowledge is expressed through obligations, permissions, prohibitions, conditions, and exceptions that require disciplined representation. Existing LLM-based extraction approaches improve scalability, but often struggle to maintain structural consistency in the generated ontology. We present a schema- and pattern-guided approach for regulatory ontology synthesis, in which LLM extraction is constrained directly within a target ontology rather than mapped to it afterward. The method incorporates human expertise through iterative prompt refinement, SHACL shape authoring, and repair-function design. It operates in two steps: structured decomposition of source text into typed components, followed by ontology generation guided by (i) a foundational schema that provides class and property whitelists, and (ii) ontology design patterns retrieved through embedding similarity to support structural alignment. A layered validation architecture combines SHACL conformance checking with SHACL-informed deterministic repair to address property-domain and type-discipline errors introduced during generation. We evaluate the approach through a multi-stage framework covering quality metrics, ontology correctness, and source-text faithfulness. The method is demonstrated on education funding regulations across four jurisdictions, showing that schema- and pattern-constrained generation supports the synthesis of structurally consistent regulatory ontologies.
